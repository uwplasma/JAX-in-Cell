# Explicit and implicit schemes

The two time integrators can be compared on any configuration by changing
`time_evolution_algorithm`. The figure below runs `examples/input.toml` with both.

```python
from jaxincell import Simulation, load_parameters, diagnostics

parameters = load_parameters("examples/input.toml")
for algorithm in (0, 1):
    parameters["solver_parameters"]["time_evolution_algorithm"] = algorithm
    output = Simulation(parameters).run()
    diagnostics(output)
    error = abs(output["total_energy"] / output["total_energy"][0] - 1)
    print(algorithm, float(error.max()))
```

The two schemes agree on the linear growth and on the saturation level. They differ in
the noise during the linear phase, because the implicit scheme does not filter the
sources, and in the energy bookkeeping: the explicit leapfrog is not exactly energy
conserving and its error oscillates and drifts, while the implicit scheme conserves the
discrete energy at every converged step. Wall-clock time per step is several times
larger for the implicit scheme with the default two sub-steps and a handful of Picard
iterations.

`examples/bump-on-tail.py` offers the same switch through `simulation_type = 2`.
