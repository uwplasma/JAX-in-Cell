# Digital filtering

The charge and current densities produced by a finite number of pseudo-particles
contain noise at all wavelengths down to the cell size. Short-wavelength noise heats
the plasma numerically through aliasing, and the S2 spline alone does not remove it.
The explicit scheme therefore smooths $\rho$ and $\mathbf J$ with a compensated
binomial filter before they enter the field equations, following Birdsall and Langdon
{cite}`birdsall1991` (appendix C).

## One pass

A pass with stride $s$ and weight $\alpha$ replaces the array $f$ by

```{math}
f_j \;\to\; \alpha f_j + \frac{1 - \alpha}{2}\left(f_{j-s} + f_{j+s}\right),
```

whose response to a Fourier mode of wavenumber $k$ is

```{math}
H_1(k) = \alpha + (1 - \alpha)\cos(k s \Delta x).
```

With $\alpha = \tfrac12$ this is the binomial (1, 2, 1)/4 smoothing for $s = 1$;
it has zero response at the Nyquist wavenumber $k\Delta x = \pi$ and a response
$1 - (k\Delta x)^2/4$ at long wavelengths.

## Passes and compensation

`filter_passes` $= p$ applies $p - 1$ passes with $\alpha$ followed by one pass with

```{math}
\alpha_c = p - \alpha\,(p - 1),
```

which is larger than one and sharpens the result. For $\alpha = \tfrac12$ the total
response is

```{math}
H_p(k) = \left[\frac{1 + \cos\theta}{2}\right]^{p-1}\left[\alpha_c + (1 - \alpha_c)\cos\theta\right], \qquad \theta = k s\Delta x,
```

whose expansion at small $\theta$ has no $\theta^2$ term: the compensation pass cancels
the long-wavelength attenuation of the binomial passes, so the resolved modes are
untouched and the cut-off is steep. The whole sequence is repeated for every stride in
`filter_strides`; the responses multiply.

Two consequences of the formula are easy to miss:

* $p = 1$ is only the compensation pass with $\alpha_c = 1$, which is the identity.
  The smallest number of passes that filters is 2.
* With the defaults ($p = 5$, strides 1, 2, 4) the response falls below $0.5$ at
  $k\Delta x \approx 0.1\pi$ and is negligible beyond $0.15\pi$. Wavelengths shorter
  than about thirteen cells are removed from the sources. A mode you want to follow
  must be longer than that: in a 70-cell box the filter removes everything above
  mode 5, which is why `examples/bump-on-tail.toml`, whose fastest mode is number 7,
  sets `filter_passes = 0`.

| setting | $H = 0.5$ at $k\Delta x/\pi$ | wavelength there (cells) | $H < 0.02$ at $k\Delta x/\pi$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 passes, strides (1, 2, 4) | 0.16 | 13 | 0.23 |
| 5 passes, strides (1, 2, 4) | 0.09 | 22 | 0.16 |
| 5 passes, strides (1, 2) | 0.18 | 11 | 0.32 |
| 5 passes, stride (1,) | 0.38 | 5 | 0.65 |

## Where it is applied

The filter acts on the total charge density used for the electrostatic solver and for
the output, and on each component of the current density, at every step of the
explicit scheme and on the initial charge density. The implicit scheme does not
filter. The boundary treatment follows the field codes, see {doc}`boundaries`.
`filter_alpha` is a differentiable input; the number of passes and the strides are
static. The number of regular passes is capped at 16 so that the loop has a fixed
length under compilation.
