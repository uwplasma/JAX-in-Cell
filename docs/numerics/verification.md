# Comparison with linear theory

The figures on this page compare simulations with the roots of the kinetic
dispersion relation for the same populations, computed with the plasma dispersion
function in `docs/scripts/dispersion.py`. The growth and damping rates are measured
with the procedure described in {doc}`diagnostics`; the numbers quoted in the text
are read from the same runs that produced the figures. They are meant to show that
the code reproduces the standard kinetic results at the resolution of the examples,
and to illustrate what limits the agreement, which is mostly the noise of a finite
number of pseudo-particles rather than the numerical scheme.

## Landau damping

A quiet start (equally spaced positions, Maxwellian velocities at the quantiles of a
bit-reversed sequence, supplied through `initial_positions` and `initial_velocities`)
with {{ landau_quiet_particles }} electrons, a displacement perturbation of relative
amplitude $ak = $ {{ landau_quiet_a_k }} and $k\lambda_D = $ {{ landau_k_lambda_D }}.

```{figure} ../_static/figures/landau_damping.png
:width: 100%
:alt: Landau damping with a quiet start

(a) Electrostatic energy with the fit through its maxima, $\gamma = $ {{ landau_gamma_measured }} $\,\omega_{pe}$,
and the kinetic root $\gamma = $ {{ landau_gamma_theory }} $\,\omega_{pe}$. (b) The first
Fourier mode of $E_x$ with the theoretical envelope; the frequency from the zero
crossings is $\omega_r = $ {{ landau_omega_measured }} $\,\omega_{pe}$ against
{{ landau_omega_theory }} $\,\omega_{pe}$. The wave decays through
{{ landau_quiet_efolds }} e-foldings before reaching the noise floor. Generated by
`docs/scripts/fig_landau_damping.py`.
```

The quiet start matters: with random velocities and 40000 particles the noise floor
is reached after one e-folding, and with the large amplitude of
`examples/Landau_damping.py` the wave is in the trapping regime and damps faster than
the linear rate (see {doc}`../examples/landau_damping`).

## Two-stream instability

The configuration of `examples/input.toml` with {{ two_stream_particles_main }}
particles per species (four times the file's value). The first mode of the box has
$k\lambda_D = $ {{ two_stream_k_lambda_D }} and $kv_d/\omega_{pe} = $ {{ two_stream_k_vd_over_wpe }};
the kinetic root is a purely growing mode with
$\gamma = $ {{ two_stream_gamma_theory }} $\,\omega_{pe}$.

```{figure} ../_static/figures/two_stream.png
:width: 100%
:alt: Two-stream instability against linear theory

(a) Electrostatic energy; the rate fitted in the shaded window, chosen from the
first Fourier mode between thirty times its initial level and three percent of its
peak, is $\gamma = $ {{ two_stream_gamma_measured_energy }} $\,\omega_{pe}$. (b) Growth
rate of the first mode against the drift speed, from ten runs of the same compiled
program with the drift passed as a runtime input; the mean deviation from theory over
the usable points is {{ two_stream_scan_mean_relative_deviation }}. (c-e) Electron
phase space. Generated by `docs/scripts/fig_two_stream.py`.
```

Near the cold-beam threshold $kv_d = \omega_{pe}$ the growth rate is sensitive to the
thermal spread and the linear phase is short, because the instability starts from
the noise of the particle sampling and saturates after a few e-foldings. The rates
fitted from runs with the 3500 particles of the input file scatter by tens of percent
around the kinetic value; the scan in panel (b) shows the agreement that the larger
particle number gives.

## Bump-on-tail instability

The four populations of `examples/bump-on-tail.toml` as written. Linear theory gives
the fastest growth for mode {{ bump_on_tail_fastest_mode }},
$\gamma = $ {{ bump_on_tail_gamma_theory }} $\,\omega_{pe}$ at
$\omega_r = $ {{ bump_on_tail_omega_theory }} $\,\omega_{pe}$. The fit of the mode's own
amplitude over the window [{{ bump_on_tail_fit_window }}] $\omega_{pe}^{-1}$ gives
{{ bump_on_tail_gamma_mode_fit }} $\,\omega_{pe}$, while the total electrostatic energy
grows at {{ bump_on_tail_gamma_energy_fit }} $\,\omega_{pe}$ because several modes of
similar growth rate develop together from noise and the fastest saturates within
about two e-foldings. The figure is on the {doc}`../examples/bump_on_tail` page.

## Weibel instability

The configuration of `examples/Weibel_instability.py` with
{{ weibel_check_particles }} particles per species, {{ weibel_check_steps }} steps at
$c\,\Delta t/\Delta x = $ {{ weibel_check_courant }} and the filter off, so that modes up
to $kc/\omega_{pe} \approx 10$ are not damped by the filter. The anisotropy is $T_z/T_x = $ {{ weibel_anisotropy }}.

```{figure} ../_static/figures/weibel.png
:width: 100%
:alt: Weibel instability against linear theory

(a) $B_y(x, t)$. (b) Magnetic energy, with the fit in the growth window of the
fastest mode and the theoretical rate of that mode,
$\gamma = $ {{ weibel_gamma_theory_max }} $\,\omega_{pe}$ at $kc/\omega_{pe} = $ {{ weibel_kc_over_wpe_fastest }}.
(c) Growth rate of each Fourier mode of $B_y$ fitted in its own window, for the
{{ weibel_modes_compared }} modes that grew by at least two e-foldings, against the
transverse dispersion relation; the mean deviation is {{ weibel_mean_relative_deviation }}.
The total energy changes by {{ weibel_energy_error }} over the run. Generated by
`docs/scripts/fig_weibel.py`.
```

Modes grow from noise at rates that differ by less than a factor two over a wide
range of $k$, so the mode-by-mode fit is the meaningful comparison; the total
magnetic energy mixes them. The saturated field organises into filaments that merge
towards longer wavelengths, as in panel (a).

## Reproducing these results

```bash
pip install scipy
python docs/scripts/make_all.py
```

regenerates every figure and `docs/_static/figures/measurements.json`. Each script is
self-contained and documents the parameters that differ from the corresponding example.
